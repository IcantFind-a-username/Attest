## 1. Price recomputed at $1.20 per review, then n

The owner asked for the price to be recomputed at $1.20 per review before n was chosen. With
zero observed errors the 95% upper bound on the per-review wrong-publication rate is
`1 - 0.05^(1/n)`, so:

| n | 95% upper bound | at the owner's **$1.20/review** | at the corpus-measured $0.2177 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | 13.29% | $25.20 | $4.57 |
| 58 | **5.03%** | $69.60 | $12.63 |
| 100 (**the target**) | 2.95% | **$120.00** | $21.77 |
| 300 | 0.99% — `G-NULL-001`'s bound | $360.00 | $65.31 |

At $1.20 a review the target of n = 100 costs $120, which is above the whole $90 cap, let alone
this window's $45. **The reservation was therefore $26** — 21 reviews guaranteed at the
conservative price, and far more at the measured one — with the driver enforcing a hard
cumulative cap and the achieved n reported rather than assumed. That is the owner's "take the
largest n within the limit".

**In the event the money was never the binding constraint** — neither the population nor the budget stopped this run. A control published, and the run stopped on the fifteenth review with $25.41 unspent. See §4 and §5.

## 3. The qualification rate is 6.4%, and that is the result nobody ordered

**826 of the 845 rejections are the untouched check**: some later commit touched a file the
commit added. The remaining 19 added no line to any text file at all.

The rule is applied as D-122 wrote it — to **every** text file the commit changed, not only its
Python. On a maintained repository that is close to unsatisfiable, and for a reason that has
nothing to do with defects: a commit that also edits `CHANGES.rst` is disqualified the moment the
changelog is rewritten or deleted, which every one of these projects has done. `click` is the
clearest case: 3 of 120.

This is why n stopped at 58 with **$25.98 of the $26 reservation unspent** (§4). The binding
constraint on `G-NULL-001a` is not the budget the owner raised; it is **the control definition
itself**. Reaching n = 100 needs roughly 650 more commits screened — about two more hours of
`git blame` — and reaching `G-NULL-001`'s n = 300 needs ~4,700, which those eight repositories
do not contain. More repositories, not more money, is the lever.

## 5. Root cause, under `RISK-CERT-01`

**The control.** `jinja` `ac3ac6c9`, 2022-03-03, *"async_variant filters are pickleable"*. It
replaces one `functools.wraps` with a stacked pair and **says in its own comment what it is
doing**:

```python
# Take the doc and annotations from the sync function, but the
# name from the async function. Pallets-Sphinx-Themes
# build_function_directive expects __wrapped__ to point to the
# sync function.
```

**What the product published.** A generated test that asserts
`do_thing_async.__name__ == "do_thing"` — the *sync* function's name. It passes on the parent
and fails on the commit, because the commit deliberately takes `__name__` from the async
function. The receipt is **valid in every mechanical sense**: head fails 3 of 3, base passes 3
of 3, in isolation, from fresh state, with the changed lines executed, and the bundle verifies
offline. The claim it carries is a defect claim about a change the author made on purpose,
documented in the same diff, unchanged for four and a half years.

The published claim is also wrong about itself. Its prose says `__wrapped__` points to the async
function; the test does not test that, asserts `__wrapped__ is do_thing`, and passes that
assertion. The ranking prose and the evidence are about different things — S proposes, V
certifies, and nothing checks that the sentence shown to the author is the sentence the test
proved.

**Why no gate stopped it.**

- The **differential kernel** did its job: head and base really do differ.
- The **binding policy** (`attest.binding.changed-line-coverage.v1`) did its job: the changed
  lines were executed.
- The **intent discriminator** (D-102, `attest.intent.v2`) had nothing to say, and the receipt
  records exactly that: `new_rejection: false`, `exception_type: ""`, `witnesses: []`. It
  classifies a head failure that a `raise` or `assert` **in the changed lines** produced. Here
  the changed code raises nothing. It returns a different string, and the assertion that fails
  is in the *generated test*.

**So the root cause is structural, not a bug.** Every rule the product has answers *"did the
behaviour change, and is the change bound to this diff?"* — and the answer here is yes, twice.
**Nothing in the chain asks whether the author meant it**, except for the one shape D-102
covers: a new rejection. An intended change of a returned *value* is invisible to every
discriminator the product owns, and it publishes.

That is the class of false publication `G-NULL-001` exists to measure, and this is the first
time it has been observed on a control that is properly qualified rather than mis-stratified.
The 2026-09-03 corpus's two control publications (`c03`, `c05`) were both adjudicated true
positives on commits that should not have been controls. **This one is not.**

## 6. What was and was not done about it

Per the owner's rule the run **stopped at once** and was root-caused before anything else was
bought. It was **not fixed and not resumed**: a discriminator that can tell an intended value
change from a regression is the same class of change as D-102 — it moves what publishes — and
D-102 was an owner decision. Inventing one at the end of a window and validating it on the
population it was designed against would be the worst of both worlds. **43 of the 58 controls
were never run**, and $25.41 of the $26 reservation is unspent.

## 7. The permitted claim, stated with its n and its bound as the gate requires

There is none. `G-NULL-001a`'s permitted claim is conditioned on zero wrong publications, and
one was observed. For the record, and **not** as a claim: on **15** preregistered qualified null
commits across **5** repositories, **1** wrong publication was observed; a 95% upper bound on the
per-review wrong-publication rate from that sample is not the `1 - 0.05^(1/n)` zero-error form at
all, and the honest reading of one error in fifteen is that the rate is somewhere around 7% with
an interval far too wide to act on.

`G-NULL-001` remains unpassed and unattempted, and is reported beside this, as instructed.

## 8. Bias, stated as the amendment requires

**The rule selects cold code.** A line untouched for six months is a line nobody had to fix
*and* a line nobody exercises. Twelve of these fifteen controls produced **no candidate at all**;
the mean spend was $0.039 against $0.22 on the real-traffic corpus. A null population made of
code the product has almost nothing to say about **biases the measured false-publication rate
downward** by an amount this design does not estimate — which makes the one publication that did
occur worse news, not better.

Two further limits: five repositories, not the thirty `G-NULL-001` asks for; and the controls
were selected by a git rule, not adjudicated defect-free by a human.
