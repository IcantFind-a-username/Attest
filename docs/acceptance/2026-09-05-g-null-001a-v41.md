## Does the gate pass? The arithmetic says yes and the method says ask

`G-NULL-001a`'s stated pass condition is *"the preregistered n is reached with zero wrong
publications, every control qualified by the amendment's two checks, every trial carrying a
replayable evidence bundle, and the repo-cluster analysis reported"*.

- **n reached:** yes, 58 of 58, one policy version.
- **zero wrong publications:** yes.
- **controls qualified:** yes, by the unchanged 2026-09-04 manifest; nothing re-sampled.
- **replayable evidence:** the run certified nothing, so it wrote **no bundles**; every trial's
  per-run evidence — head and base outcomes, stdout, exit codes, network flag — is in its clone's
  ledger, and the four discriminator decisions carry their full intent records there.
- **repo-cluster analysis reported:** 8 repositories, 8 clusters, **below the ~10 the gate itself
  calls uninformative.** Reported, not passed.

**And the objection that outranks all five: this population is no longer independent of the rule
it is testing.** `jinja ac3ac6c9` is the commit D-127 was written to stop and `urllib3 c7b9adcb`
is the commit D-132 was written to stop; both are *in the 58*. The population, cutoff, seed and
quota were never touched — the discipline held — but the rule has now been revised twice against
these very controls, so **5.2% is an optimistic bound and this run is a regression test, not a
fresh null study.**

**I do not claim the gate passes. The arithmetic pass condition is met; whether that counts is
the owner's call**, and the two answers cost different things:

- **"It passes."** The product may state a ≤ 5.2% upper bound on wrong publications over the
  amended control definition, naming n = 58 and this population's non-independence in the same
  sentence.
- **"It does not, until a fresh population."** A second, disjoint sample from the same eight
  repositories under the same cutoff, seed-shifted, would cost about **$1.10** at this run's
  measured price — the cheapest independent evidence this gate has ever had available.

## Cost, against a reservation that was set the way the owner asked

The reservation basis was changed to **the single-unit maximum, not the mean**, after a $0.30
reservation was overrun by one $0.4045 control. At $4.00 it held with room: **$1.0735 spent,
$2.9265 released.** The mean was $0.0185 — 43% below the $0.033 of the earlier runs, because
prompt caching now covers most reviews — and the maximum was $0.3896, the same control as last
time and again more than ten times the mean. **The basis change was right and the numbers say so:
a mean-based $1.92 reservation would have been fine here, but a single control can still eat a
fifth of it.**
